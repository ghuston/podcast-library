Executive summary — the one-pager

Read this in a couple of minutes. It is concepts only. Every figure lives in the detail sections below.

  • None of these four is a CRM in the same sense as Zoho CRM. They are booking, scheduling, payment, and clinical or coaching record systems. A CRM's job is the relationship before someone becomes a client and between visits: who asked, when, what was said, what happened next, and whether the money arrived. Booking software records what was booked and paid. Different jobs.
  • The answer is almost always "alongside", never "switch". The incumbent holds the bookings, the payments, the client history, and often the legal or clinical record. 1. The ELI5 frame: the front desk, the memory, and the join

Every analogy in this report uses one picture. A wellness business has three things software can be:

  • The front desk. The booking system. It knows the schedule, the rooms, the classes, who is booked, who paid, and who showed up. Mindbody, Glofox, Trainerize, and SimplePractice are all, first, front desks — each with a different kind of desk. The front desk is excellent at today. It is bad at before today (who asked and never booked?) and across (did the money from last month actually arrive, and from which channel?).
  • The memory. The CRM. A CRM remembers every person, every conversation, every promise, and every follow-up that is owed — including the people who never became clients. It does not book the class. It makes sure nobody is forgotten. Zoho CRM is a memory. Zoho Bookings, note, is a front desk, not a memory — and it is a separate Zoho product with a separate subscription.
  • The join. The reporting layer that puts the front desk, the memory, the bank, and the marketing spend on one page and answers the owner's real question: "Am I making money, from whom, and where is it leaking?" Zoho Analytics, or a small Zoho Creator app, can be the join. No booking system is the join, because no booking system sees the bank or the ad spend.

The pushback Grant hears — "we already have software" — is almost always a front-desk answer to a memory question. The owner is right that they have a front desk. They are wrong that the front desk remembers. Grant's job in the room is to show, from the owner's own counts, the people the front desk forgot and the money it cannot see. That is the five-gaps diagnostic, and it is the product's front door.

System of record vs system of engagement, in plain words. A system of record is the ledger: it writes down what happened, and it is the place you go when two numbers disagree. A system of engagement is where the work happens: the calls, the texts, the follow-ups. In a car dealership, the DMS is the record (cars, deals, repair orders, money) and the CRM is the engagement (leads, follow-up tasks, emails). In Grant's world, the booking system is the operational record for bookings and payments, and Zoho CRM is the engagement layer for the relationship. Most small businesses run both, and integrate them. Neither replaces the other, and confusing them is how consultants lose the room.

2. What a CRM is — and what Zoho CRM costs

A CRM (customer relationship management) system answers: who is this person, how did they find us, what did we say, what is the next step, and did it happen? Its core objects are leads (people who have not bought yet), contacts, deals or opportunities with stages and values, activities (calls, emails, tasks), and the full timeline of a relationship. It does not, by itself, book a class, take a payment, or hold a clinical note.

Zoho CRM pricing, US list prices checked by third parties against Zoho's own pricing page in Oct–Nov 2026: free for up to 3 users; Standard $14, Professional $23, Enterprise $40, Ultimate $52 per user per month, billed annually (monthly billing higher: roughly $20/$35/$50/$65). Professional adds Blueprint process management, CPQ, inventory, and customer journeys. Enterprise adds Zia AI, sandbox, approvals, territories. For a small studio with one owner and one front-desk user, Standard at $14/user/month is the realistic starting point — about $28/month for two seats, before tax. That number matters in the room: the CRM is not the expensive part. The expensive part is the work Grant does on top of it.

Zoho Creator, Flow, Analytics — the extension layer. Zoho Creator is a low-code platform for building small custom apps (forms, reports, workflows, Deluge code), priced $8 (Standard) / $20 (Professional) / $25 (Enterprise) per user per month billed annually. Creator 6 bundles BI & Analytics (powered by Zoho Analytics) and Integration Flow (powered by Zoho Flow) on Enterprise, with 600+ third-party connectors claimed. Zoho Flow is the glue: when X happens in one app, do Y in another. Zoho Analytics is the join: pull data from several systems into one dashboard. The honest framing for a client: Creator builds the missing screen (for example an arrears board), Flow moves the data, Analytics shows the owner the truth. All three need the data to actually arrive — which is why the API and export section below decides what is possible per platform.

Zoho Bookings is not a CRM. It starts at $6/user/month and handles scheduling only; it needs a separate Zoho CRM subscription to track relationships and pipelines, and its sync to CRM is one-way in places (bookings sync to CRM, but appointments booked in Zoho Bookings do not always appear as CRM activities). Do not let a client, or Grant himself, confuse "we use Zoho" with "we have a CRM".

3. Mindbody — the most open front desk

What it is. The biggest fitness and wellness booking platform: class and appointment scheduling, memberships and packages, payments (Mindbody Payments, built on Stripe Connect), a client app and marketplace listing, marketing tools, and 100+ pre-built reports. Pricing: "starting at $79/month per location" is the only published number; Starter, Accelerate, and Ultimate are all quote-only. Owner-reported figures (secondhand, not list prices): Starter roughly $99–$159/month, Accelerate roughly $259–$279, Ultimate $499+ per location. Processing is about 2.75% per transaction (third-party reports; Mindbody does not publish the percentage). A new client whose first purchase comes through the Mindbody app carries a 20% marketplace fee, capped at $30.

What it does well. The deepest reporting of the four (100+ reports), recurring billing with automatic retry on failed payments, a branded booking widget, and a real developer ecosystem. Ultimate adds a lead management dashboard, a sales pipeline, automated email and text campaigns, and AI Concierge — in other words Mindbody itself sells a CRM-flavoured layer at the top tier. That matters for Grant's pitch: on Ultimate, the owner already bought a pipeline; the gap is whether anyone works it.

What it does NOT do. It does not join to the bank, the ad platforms, or the owner's rent and payroll, so it cannot show profit per instructor or CAC. Its reporting is per-system. And the arrears trap: prior research in this series verified in Mindbody's own support docs that a declined autopay converted to a negative balance shows as "Successful" on the Autopay Detail report, and failed ACH payments never appear there at all — arrears only surface in the Account Balances report (negative-only, aged 0–29 / 30–59 / 60–89 / 90+ days). The ChatGPT guide's correction log marks this "unresolved — do not use in sales", because it treated our summary as an inherited claim and did not re-verify the docs. Status: re-verify in Mindbody's support docs before using or dropping it. It is too valuable to drop on a technicality and too specific to assert without a fresh check.

API and data exit — ALONGSIDE, and genuinely so. Mindbody has a Public API v6 (REST, JSON, OAuth 2.0) that can read and write bookings, clients, payments, class schedules, staff, and memberships (verified-live via Mindbody's Developer Portal FAQ and support docs in search). Access path: create source credentials in the Developer Portal, build in a free sandbox, complete a GoLive application for live data, and each client site must be activated by the business owner via an activation link or code on the API Integrations screen. Free API access for developers under 5,000 calls per billing cycle; a third-party skill records 1,000 requests/hour per API key (unverified — treat as directional). Webhooks require Partner Program enrolment for production; without it, polling every 5–15 minutes is the fallback. On leaving, Mindbody offers a Subscriber Data Export, but it is tied to cancellation (request within 25 days, access ends at 30 days) — a fact worth telling a client before they ever think about switching.

Verdict: ALONGSIDE, with real extension possible. Never switch. The booking engine, the payments, and the marketplace listing stay. Zoho CRM becomes the lead pipeline and follow-up engine fed by the Mindbody API (or by exports on lower tiers), and a small Creator or Analytics join shows the owner profit per instructor and arrears ageing. The API makes this the only platform of the four where a genuinely live join is buildable — and even here, the GoLive plus per-site activation steps mean Grant should budget a setup week, not an afternoon.

4. Glofox (ABC Glofox) — the confusing one

What it is. Gym and studio management software (acquired by ABC Fitness in 2023, rebranded ABC Glofox), built for class-based studios and gyms: scheduling and booking, memberships and credit packs, recurring billing and payments, a custom branded member app, CRM and marketing, reporting, access control and check-in, staff management, and multi-location tools. Its own features page, read live on 7 Nov 2026, says it is "trusted by 30,000+ studios and gyms worldwide" and lists "CRM & Marketing: capture leads everywhere, then convert them with automated email, SMS, and push journeys", "automated failed-payment recovery", and "revenue, retention, and performance reporting in one dashboard — with AI churn prediction built in". Pricing is quote-only; owner-reported figures (secondhand): Standard roughly $80–$110/month, mid-tier $190–$250, top tiers $600+/month, billed quarterly with annual contracts in many regions.

What it does well. The branded member app is its headline differentiator (members book through the studio's own app, not a shared marketplace). Member management shows at-risk members, expiring memberships, and who owes money. Third-party feature checks record a Failed Payment Report, automated retries, payment links for failed subscriptions, a Money Owed report, and sales and transactions reporting. That is more collections visibility out of the box than Mindbody gives — which narrows, but does not erase, Grant's payment-chasedown gap: the reports exist, the question is whether anyone works them weekly.

What it does NOT do. Reporting depth is its weak spot by third-party consensus: attendance, revenue, retention, membership breakdowns, but thin on deep financial analysis, instructor performance comparison, and pricing or scheduling optimisation. Advanced lead nurture lives behind Amplify, a paid CRM add-on — the base CRM is capture plus basic journeys. No P&L, no CAC (it holds no marketing spend), no cross-system join.

API and data exit — ALONGSIDE, via the side door. This is the platform where sources directly conflict, and the conflict itself is the finding. Glofox's own features page, read live, says the platform "offers an API and webhooks for custom workflows" and runs "an enterprise API" for multi-location and franchise operations. Third-party directories (SoftwareWorld, Slashdot, SourceForge) say flatly "No API" / "API: Not available". The honest reconciliation: there is no public, self-serve API a small studio or consultant can sign up for and read docs for; an enterprise API exists behind a sales conversation. For Grant's purposes, treat Glofox as API-closed unless the client is on an enterprise tier and can get the API in writing. The practical integration routes are Zapier (listed among its integrations, alongside Stripe, GoCardless, Mailchimp, HubSpot, Facebook Pixel, and Trainerize), Mailchimp, and exports. No public API docs were found; what data exports cover could not be verified — flag that as an open check on the first Glofox client (ask for a sample export before scoping any join).

Verdict: ALONGSIDE, and start with the native tool. Glofox already has a CRM section, a Money Owed report, and failed-payment retries. Grant's first paid job on a Glofox studio may be configuring what they already pay for — the Amplify question, the weekly arrears routine, the lead journeys — and only then a Zoho layer for the cross-system view (CAC, profit per instructor, lead source to revenue). Switch: no. The branded app alone is a reason the owner will not move, and they are right.

5. Trainerize (ABC Trainerize) — the gated one

What it is. Coaching software for personal trainers and fitness coaches: client programs, workouts, nutrition and habit tracking, messaging, appointments, and payments (via Stripe Integrated Payments, $10/month add-on). It is not a class-booking system; it is a 1:1 and small-group coaching app. Pricing published (third-party checks, Oct 2026): paid plans from $10/month (2 clients), $50 at 10 clients, $79 at 20, $135 at 50; Studio Plus $275/month per location (500 clients). The $25/month Business bundle covers payments, video, and prospect booking links. Trainerize has no website, funnel, or email marketing tools — those stay as separate subscriptions.

What it does well. The coaching workflow itself: programs, progress tracking, messaging, and wearable data. Prospect booking links and referrals exist on higher plans. For a PT business, it is the front desk and the coaching floor in one app.

What it does NOT do. No class scheduling at scale, no P&L, no CAC, no cross-system reporting, no lead pipeline worthy of the name on lower plans. The Business add-on's prospect booking is a link, not a pipeline with first-response stamping and follow-up cadence.

API and data exit — ALONGSIDE, on high plans only. Trainerize's own help centre (verified-live via search): "The ABC Trainerize API allows you to create direct connections between ABC Trainerize and other applications… Most of the actions that are possible in the ABC Trainerize User Interface are possible through our API." Access: Studio or Enterprise plans, or the FitMetrics add-on. Limits, from the same help page: historical lookback 180 days; 250,000 calls per month; 1,000 requests per minute per Group API token. Docs are not public — you email support for a token. Webhooks only on Studio and Enterprise. Zapier works on any plan and needs no direct API access. Exports are the weak point: client contact details as CSV and workout and program PDFs are exportable, but workout history, progress stats, and measurements cannot be exported, and exports exclude messages, photos, and attachments. A trainer's most valuable asset — the client's progress history — does not leave the system in usable form. That is a fact to put in front of a client before any talk of switching, and a reason the alongside model is the only honest one.

Verdict: ALONGSIDE. Zoho CRM holds the pipeline, the lead follow-up, and the client lifecycle outside the coaching app; Trainerize keeps the coaching. On Studio or Enterprise, a live-ish API join is possible; on Pro and below, Zapier plus exports. Switch: no — the progress history cannot be carried out, so a switch would destroy the trainer's own record.

6. SimplePractice — the closed one, and Grant's proven model

What it is. Practice management software and EHR for therapists and health and wellness professionals: scheduling, intake documents, clinical notes, telehealth, insurance claims, billing, and a client portal. Pricing from prior research (third-party summaries, re-verify at proposal time): Starter $49 / Essential $79 / Plus $99 per month for the first clinician; group Plus adds about $74/month per additional clinician (2–5) and $72 (6–15). Card processing 3.15% + $0.30; claims $0.39/claim from 1 Oct 2026.

What it does well — verified live from its own support docs (5 Nov 2026, prior research). Practice Pulse (Plus only, daily refresh, fixed timeframes) shows completed sessions, cancel and no-show rate, retention, income, average income per session, and goals. Reports include Income, Income allocation by clinician (Plus, for payroll), Referral income (lifetime revenue by source, with a date-filter quirk: it populates by the date the client was added), claims, balances, and attendance. The Oct 2026 announcement added 15+ analytics metrics on Plus at no extra charge. And native Inquiries (updated 29 July 2026, per the Drive guide) organises prospective clients, source, intake progress, tasks, and clinician matching — which materially weakens any blanket claim that a practice "has no intake pipeline".

What it does NOT do — the genuine gaps, verified. No P&L or profit after clinician pay and overhead. No marketing spend or CAC. No pre-client inquiry pipeline with response-time tracking (Inquiries organises prospects who are already in the system; it does not timestamp first response across email, Psychology Today, Google Business, voicemail, and DMs, and it does not hold people who never became clients in a follow-up sequence). No cross-system owner view. And the data-hygiene gap: Referral income only works if someone enters the source consistently, which in most practices they do not.

API and data exit — ALONGSIDE, by export. SimplePractice's own support page, read in search on 7 Nov 2026: "SimplePractice doesn't currently offer a public-facing API for integrations with other software or platforms." That is the vendor's own words, and it is the safe statement for a sales conversation. One third-party source (a GitHub skill file) claims a full public OAuth API is in partner-program rollout as of early 2026, with customer-side scheduled CSV exports as the fallback. Status: conflicting and unconfirmed — do not sell against the partner API, and do not promise it. For a small practice today, data leaves via reports exported as CSV or Excel, some reports schedulable for generation but not automatic download. The flagship "live join into Zoho Analytics" is really a scheduled owner-run export-to-import. Never promise live sync. That changes the product: the export-based refresh becomes the concrete value of the Care retainer.

HIPAA note. A BAA is required before any PHI flows; Zoho offers a BAA template on request (legal@zohocorp.com), and Zoho CRM is HIPAA-supporting only when configured, only for BAA-covered services. The CRM holds pipeline data only — no diagnoses, no notes. Discovery uses aggregates, counts, and rates, never patient exports.

Verdict: ALONGSIDE — and this is Grant's proven model. With therapy clients he already used Zoho alongside SimplePractice, not as a replacement. That is not a compromise; it is the answer. SimplePractice keeps the clinical record, the claims, and the billing. Zoho CRM holds the intake pipeline across every channel, stamps first-response time, runs the follow-up cadence, and joins the SimplePractice numbers to marketing spend, rent, and payroll so the owner can finally see profit per clinician. Switch: no — and for therapy, switching an EHR is a clinical and compliance project, not a software preference.

8. The sales question — how to sell when they already like their software

Grant's framing: this is a SALES question, not a business-model decision about working with vs against these systems. The key insight: a small-scope fix project does not make sense if it means switching the client from Glofox (or Mindbody, or SimplePractice) to Zoho — that is a bigger project. So the episode answers how he sells small, valuable fixes that do NOT require switching.

Against them — do not. Attacking the tool the owner already pays for and likes loses the room in five minutes. Every prior report in this series says the same thing, and the Drive guide makes it operational: check the customer's current tools before prescribing another system; the first paid job may be configuring the native tool. "Sell around SimplePractice, not against it" generalises to all four platforms. The owner did not buy the wrong software. They bought a front desk and were sold it as a whole business.

With them — yes, as a starting posture, but not as the business. "With them" means configuring the native tool first: SimplePractice Inquiries set up properly; Glofox's Money Owed report worked weekly and the Amplify question answered; Mindbody's Ultimate lead dashboard actually used; Trainerize's Business add-on prospect links set up. This is real, billable, low-risk work — and it is also the fastest way to earn the right to propose the Zoho layer. But native-tool configuration alone is not a business: it is a one-off, it has no recurring revenue, and any competent admin can do it. It is the door, not the house.

Alongside them — this is the business. Zoho CRM next to the incumbent, holding what the incumbent cannot hold: the intake pipeline across every channel with first-response time stamped; the follow-up cadence (auto-acknowledgement in minutes, human reply within one business hour, the five-touch studio sequence or Day 1-3-7-14 therapy cadence); the join of bookings + payments + marketing spend + rent + payroll into profit per clinician, CAC, and lifetime value; and the arrears board the booking system hides. The alongside model is also the honest answer to the API question: where an API exists, the join can be near-live; where it does not, the join is a scheduled export, and the Care retainer is what keeps it fresh. Grant already proved this model with therapy clients and SimplePractice. It transfers to all four platforms with only the plumbing changing.

Extend them — yes, selectively, as the second sale. Once the alongside layer is working and the client trusts the numbers, small custom builds become sellable: a lead-response flow in Zoho Flow that fires on a new inquiry from any channel; an arrears board in Zoho Creator that lists who owes what, aged, with the next action; a cross-system dashboard in Zoho Analytics. The extension is priced as a sprint, not a product — the guide's 20-hour cap is the right discipline — and it is only sold after the same problem has appeared in at least three clients, so Grant is not building a product nobody asked for twice.

The recommendation, stated plainly (sales framing): agree with the objection first — "good, keep it" — then show the leak in dollars from the owner's own counts. Sell the diagnostic, then one named sprint (lead response or payment chasedown) built alongside the incumbent, at the guide's $3,000 / 20-hour cap as a launch test. Nothing moves: no switch, no new system to learn, no history to migrate. Keep the raise trigger written down: after 3–5 paid diagnostics plus a case study with a dollar figure, the diagnostic moves to $1,500–$2,500 and implementation toward $5k–$15k. The Drive guide's prices ($450 review, $3,000 sprint, no raise path, no implementation credit) are a launch test, not the settled ladder — the guide lowered the prices Grant had just been given, and its own archive prices the same rungs at roughly double. Keep the guide's discipline (hard caps, deposits, native-tool-first, one outcome per sprint) and discard its price level as a standing rate.

9. What to say in the room — the pushback, answered in plain words

  • "We already have Mindbody / Glofox / Trainerize / SimplePractice." "Good — keep it. That is your front desk, and it is doing its job. What it does not do is remember the people who asked and never booked, or show you whether the money from last month actually arrived. I am not here to replace your front desk. I am here to give it a memory."
  • "Is Zoho going to replace our software?" "No. Zoho sits next to it. Your bookings stay where they are. Zoho holds the pipeline, the follow-ups, and the one page that shows whether you are making money."
  • "Why can't our software do that itself?" "Some of it it can — and where it can, I will set that up first, because it is cheaper for you. What it cannot do is see outside itself: your ad spend, your rent, your payroll, and the leads that never made it into the system at all."
  • "Will this be a live sync?" "Only where the platform allows it. Mindbody, yes, through its API. SimplePractice, no — there the numbers refresh on a schedule, and keeping that fresh is part of the monthly care. I will tell you which one you are before you pay anything."
  • "What does it cost?" "The review that finds the leak is $450 as a launch price — and the price goes up once I have the case studies to prove it. The fix is a fixed-price sprint, capped at 20 hours, with half up front. You will know the price before I start, and you will know the number it has to beat."
